Transcript — Interview with James Aldridge, Senior Partner
Date: 2024-01-15
Interviewer: Priya (research lead)
Topic: extracting tacit knowledge from senior partners — engagement patterns

[recording starts mid-sentence]

JAMES: ...so yeah, anyway. The thing nobody documents is when to walk away from an engagement. Everyone documents how to win them. Nobody documents the moment you realise you should never have signed it.

PRIYA: Can you give me an example?

JAMES: Sure. Riverbend in 2019. Looked perfect on paper. The CEO was charming, they had budget, they had an obvious problem. We did Discovery, we did Diagnostic, scores looked workable — Strategy Clarity was a 2 but we figured we could fix that, that's literally the work. Three weeks into Transformation we realised the CEO had no intention of changing anything. He'd hired us to point at the report when the board got angry. We were a deflection.

PRIYA: How did you spot it?

JAMES: Three signals. First, he kept rescheduling the partner reviews — and not for real reasons, for vanity-meeting reasons. Second, every recommendation we made, his answer was "great, can you take that to [some VP]" — never "I'll do that." Delegating the action away from himself. And third — this is the big one — he wouldn't let us interview his CFO. Said the CFO was too busy. The CFO had three hours a week with him in 1:1s. He wasn't too busy. He was a witness.

PRIYA: And what did you do?

JAMES: We finished the engagement. Honestly we should have killed it. We took the money. I'm not proud of that. Since then I've added a check at the end of week 2 of any Transformation: am I working with this CEO, or am I working *for* them while they hide? If it's the latter, we exit, refund the unused weeks, and walk. We've done it twice since 2020. Both times the CEO was furious for about six months, then called me back, twice, to thank me.

PRIYA: That's something we should write down.

JAMES: I know. I never have. Maya has been chasing me to.

PRIYA: What else?

JAMES: Pricing. Look, I know the handbook says Discovery is loss-leading. It's not. It's loss-leading on partner hours but it's the most leveraged sales tool we have. If a client doesn't sign for Diagnostic after Discovery, we did Discovery wrong. The metric should be Discovery-to-Diagnostic conversion rate, not Discovery margin. We hit something like 78% conversion when I last looked. Industry average for this kind of work is sub 50.

PRIYA: That should be in the handbook.

JAMES: Yeah. So should this — the 14-day refund window? It exists because of one engagement in 2018 where a client realised in week 2 they didn't actually have authority to spend the money. It wasn't bad faith, it was a genuine mistake. We refunded everything and they came back two years later as a Transformation client. That's why we keep the 14-day window. It's not a financial product, it's a reputation product.

PRIYA: [laughs] You should do this monthly.

JAMES: Yeah. Yeah, I should. The other thing — the Embed stage. Everyone underestimates Embed. When we sell the engagement, clients always want to cut Embed. They see it as the dessert course they don't need. But the actual data — and Maya has the spreadsheet, I think — when we've allowed clients to skip Embed, the work erodes inside three months. When we've insisted on Embed, the work compounds. The compound effect is enormous. And here's the bit I've never said out loud — I think Embed should cost more than Transformation, not less. The value sits there. The market hasn't priced it yet.

PRIYA: That's a different business model.

JAMES: It's a hard sell. I know.

[recording cuts]
